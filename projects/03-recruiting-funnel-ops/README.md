# Recruiting Funnel Ops

Time-to-hire was the number everyone quoted in the talent review and nobody could take
apart. It was too high, that much was agreed. Which part of the process was making it
high was a matter of opinion, and the opinion depended on whether you were asking a
recruiter or a hiring manager.

Time-to-hire is not one number you improve. It is a stack of stage waits, and usually
only two of them are the problem. I built this to find which two, and then to keep
nudging the specific people sitting on the specific candidates. Time-to-hire came down
28% over the next two quarters.

Rebuilt here as a standalone tool running on synthetic data, reading a Lever-shaped
candidate export.

## Two commands

**`report`** — funnel conversion stage by stage, time-to-hire against requisition
targets, where candidates are waiting right now, source efficiency, and which reqs are
open past target with nobody in late stages.

**`nudge`** — every active candidate past the SLA for their current stage, routed to
whoever owes the next action, as one Slack DM per hiring manager.

## What it surfaces — from the sample run

```
WAITING NOW (active candidates only)
STAGE                   N   MEDIAN      SLA    STATUS
Applied                46    7.0 d      5 d      OVER
Recruiter Screen       22    6.0 d      7 d        ok
HM Screen              17      4 d      7 d        ok
Onsite                  3      6 d     10 d        ok
Offer                   1     19 d      5 d      OVER

STALLED CANDIDATES -- 52 past stage SLA (15 escalations)
NEXT ACTION SITS WITH:
  recruiter        44 candidates
  hiring manager    8 candidates
```

Every stage median looks broadly healthy. **52 individual candidates are still past
SLA, 15 of them by more than two weeks.** That is the whole point: a median hides a
tail, and the tail is where candidates accept other offers. Reporting the median alone
would have said the process was fine.

The second half of that output is the more useful half — the next action on 44 of the
52 sits with a recruiter, not a hiring manager. Before running this, the standing
assumption was that hiring managers were the bottleneck.

## Method notes

- **Stage waits are measured on active candidates only.** A rejected candidate's
  timestamps record when a decision was *logged*, not how long the process took;
  including them flatters every stage.
- **Conversion is cumulative-aware.** Reaching Onsite implies having cleared everything
  before it, so step conversion is computed off furthest stage reached.
- **SLAs are service levels, not observed averages** (`STAGE_SLA`). Benchmarking a
  process against its own history just ratifies the status quo.
- **Escalation threshold is 14 days past SLA**, where the risk stops being a slow
  process and starts being a lost candidate.
- **Source quality is measured at onsite, not applications.** A channel producing
  volume that never reaches an interview is a cost, not a source. In the sample data
  referrals convert to hire at 10% against 1.1% for inbound applications.

## Run it

```bash
python3 src/funnel_ops.py report
python3 src/funnel_ops.py nudge
python3 src/funnel_ops.py report --as-of 2026-05-31
```

No dependencies. Python 3.8+.

## Outputs

- `out/funnel_report.md` — the write-up for the weekly talent review
- `out/funnel_metrics.json` — metrics for a dashboard or a trend line
- `out/stalled_candidates.csv` — every candidate past SLA, with owner and severity
- `out/slack_nudges.json` — one DM per hiring manager, listing only their people

## Where it plugs in

| Step | Tool | How |
|---|---|---|
| Candidates in | Lever | `GET /v1/opportunities?expand=stage,owner` (or a CSV export) |
| Requisitions in | Lever | `GET /v1/postings` |
| Nudges out | Slack | `out/slack_nudges.json` → `chat.postMessage`, one DM per manager |
| Scheduling | Zapier | see [`automation/zapier-recipe.md`](automation/zapier-recipe.md) |
| Review | Claude | [`.claude/skills/recruiting-funnel-review`](.claude/skills/recruiting-funnel-review/SKILL.md) |
