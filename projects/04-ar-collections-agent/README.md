# AR & Collections Agent

Invoicing went out on time. Collections was whatever anyone got around to — which
meant it happened in the last week of the quarter, on the largest balances, by whoever
was least busy. Aged invoices were a surprise every time somebody looked.

Getting delinquency to zero was not a heroic month of phone calls. It was building a
cadence that runs on every invoice, every week, without anyone deciding whether today
is a chasing day, and then not skipping it. This is that cadence.

Rebuilt here as a standalone tool running on synthetic data.

## Two commands

**`aging`** — AR aging buckets, DSO, delinquency rate, on-time payment rate, and the
concentration analysis that answers the real question: is this a collections problem,
or three difficult customers?

**`dunning`** — builds the collections queue. Every overdue invoice lands in an
escalation tier, drafts are generated per tier, and the exceptions are pulled out
before anything sends.

## The design principle: automate the cadence, never the judgement

Three rules decide what the agent will *not* touch:

- **Disputed invoices leave the dunning cadence entirely.** Chasing payment on an
  invoice the customer has already told you is wrong destroys the relationship and
  fixes nothing. They route to dispute resolution.
- **Balances over $100K get a call before any automated email.** At that size the
  email is not the point; the relationship and the reason are.
- **Seven-day contact cooldown per account.** Two automated chasers in one week reads
  as a broken system, and it is.

In the sample ledger that holds back 5 invoices worth $147.7K — including a $109.6K
balance that would otherwise have received a form email.


That last line is the one that changes the plan. Seven in every ten overdue dollars
sit with five accounts, so the answer is five named conversations — not a company-wide payment
terms review, and not more reminder emails to everyone.

## The escalation ladder

| Days overdue | Tier | Channel | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–14 | reminder | automated email | AR owner |
| 15–30 | second notice | email, account team copied | AR owner |
| 31–60 | escalation | phone call, renewal flagged to CS | AR owner |
| 61+ | collections review | manual — payment plan, credit hold, or write-off | Finance lead |

Tone escalates with age, and the 15+ day template explicitly invites the customer to
raise a dispute instead of staying silent. An invoice stuck behind a genuine problem
should become a dispute in week three, not an unexplained 90+ balance in month four.

## Run it

```bash
python3 src/ar_agent.py aging
python3 src/ar_agent.py dunning
python3 src/ar_agent.py aging --as-of 2026-03-31
```

No dependencies. Python 3.8+.

## Outputs

- `out/ar_metrics.json` — aging, DSO, delinquency and segment splits for reporting
- `out/dunning_queue.csv` — every overdue invoice with tier, owner, and whether it is
  cleared for automation
- `out/email_drafts.md` — drafts per tier, for review before sending
- `out/slack_digest.json` — the finance-ops digest and the call list

## Where it plugs in

| Step | Tool | How |
|---|---|---|
| Invoices in | Zuora / NetSuite | AR aging export or `GET /v1/invoices` |
| Contacts | Salesforce / HubSpot | billing contact and account owner lookup |
| Sending | Gmail / Outlook | drafts created, never auto-sent |
| Digest | Slack | `out/slack_digest.json` → `#finance-ops` |
| Scheduling | Zapier | see [`automation/zapier-recipe.md`](automation/zapier-recipe.md) |
